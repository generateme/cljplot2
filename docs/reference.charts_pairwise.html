21  Pairwise statistics

These charts draw one statistic of two variables as a single glyph or cell filling the panel: the correlation (correlation) or any other pairwise statistic (pairwise). They’re the cells of a scatter-plot matrix (plot/pairs).

(ns reference.charts-pairwise
  (:require [cljplot2.chart :as chart]
            [cljplot2.core :as plot]
            [cljplot2.describe :as describe]
            [notebook-utils :as nu]))

21.1 Correlation cells

chart/correlation draws the correlation coefficient r of two variables as one glyph filling the panel. Use it as the cell of a correlogram (plot/pairs); by default it writes r, a line per group.

Shared options – groups of options other charts take too
  • Common options – :data :mapping :legend
  • Named theme styles – :theme-style
Mapping – the row values the chart reads, under :mapping
  • :x required – The first variable's values.
  • :y required – The second variable's values.
  • :color – Splits the rows into groups, each a line of the :number text in its own colour.
  • :group – Splits the rows into groups that look the same, each a line of the :number text.
(plot/render-image {:data iris
                    :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length :color :species}
                    :charts [(chart/correlation)]}
                   {:width 450 :height 300})

Its options pick the coefficient and how the chart draws it:

:colorDefault: [0 114 178] (theme); The theme's colour only for a glyph when the plot maps an aesthetic; otherwise a glyph shows r on a diverging colour scale. :number takes the :correlation text role's colour, a group line its group's colour (the text's colour for :group).
(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle, :color :orange})
One fixed colour for the glyph or the :number text, in place of the colour scale.
  • solid colour
:stroke-colorDefault: :darker (theme)
(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle, :stroke-color :none})
The outline colour of the :square, :circle, :ellipse and :pie glyphs.
  • :darker – The fill colour, darkened.
  • :none – No outline.
  • solid colour – Any other colour, e.g. :black.
:stroke-widthDefault: 1 (theme)
(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle, :stroke-width 4})
The outline width of the :square, :circle, :ellipse and :pie glyphs in pixels; 0 draws no outline.
  • non-negative number
:methodDefault: :pearson
(chart/correlation {:method :spearman})
Which correlation coefficient the chart computes as r.
  • :pearson – Pearson correlation.
  • :spearman – Spearman rank correlation.
  • :kendall – Kendall rank correlation (tau).
:glyphDefault: :number
(chart/correlation {:glyph :shade})
How the chart draws r.
  • :number – As text, the method and r, a line per group.
  • :shade – The whole panel in r's colour.
  • :square – A square with its area proportional to |r|.
  • :circle – A circle with its area proportional to |r|.
  • :ellipse – The normal contour of r: a circle at 0, narrower towards ±1, rising for a positive r.
  • :pie – A disk filled |r| of the way round, clockwise for a positive r.
:glyph-sizeDefault: 0.9
(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle, :glyph-size 0.5})
The size of the :square, :circle, :ellipse and :pie glyphs, as a fraction of the panel's shorter side.
  • fraction

Each of the six glyphs shows the same pair of variables (r about 0.82):

(plot/render-image (plot/arrange [{:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :number})] :labels {:title ":glyph :number"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :shade})] :labels {:title ":glyph :shade"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :square})] :labels {:title ":glyph :square"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle})] :labels {:title ":glyph :circle"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :ellipse})] :labels {:title ":glyph :ellipse"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :pie})] :labels {:title ":glyph :pie"}}]
                                 {:columns 3})
                   {:width 900 :height 560})

The three coefficients differ for the same pair (Kendall’s tau, which compares concordant with discordant pairs, is the lowest). On the right a smaller glyph has one fixed colour and a thick outline:

(plot/render-image (plot/arrange [{:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:method :pearson})] :labels {:title ":method :pearson"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:method :spearman})] :labels {:title ":method :spearman"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:method :kendall})] :labels {:title ":method :kendall"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-width}
                                   :charts [(chart/correlation {:glyph :circle :glyph-size 0.6 :color [120 160 90]
                                                                :stroke-color :black :stroke-width 3})]
                                   :labels {:title ":glyph-size 0.6"}}]
                                 {:columns 4})
                   {:width 1000 :height 280})

A correlogram is a plot/pairs matrix of these cells: here ellipses below the diagonal (:lower), Spearman squares above it (:upper) and the variables’ names on it (:diagonal :names):

(plot/render-image (plot/pairs iris {:variables [:sepal-length :sepal-width :petal-length :petal-width]
                                     :lower (chart/correlation {:glyph :ellipse :stroke-color :none})
                                     :upper (chart/correlation {:glyph :square :method :spearman})
                                     :diagonal :names
                                     :title "ellipses (Pearson) below, squares (Spearman) above"})
                   {:width 700 :height 620})

21.2 Statistic cells

chart/pairwise draws a statistic of two variables as one cell filling the panel: an error, an association, an effect size, a distance or a function of the two samples, always of the x sample against the y sample. Use it as the cell of a plot/pairs matrix; by default it writes the value, a line per group. Unlike r the values have no fixed range. The pairwise/matrix-rows section lists the built-in statistics.

Shared options – groups of options other charts take too
  • Common options – :data :mapping :legend
  • Named theme styles – :theme-style
Mapping – the row values the chart reads, under :mapping
  • :x required – The x sample, the statistic's xs.
  • :y required – The y sample, the statistic's ys.
  • :color – Splits the rows into groups, each a line of the :number text in its own colour.
  • :group – Splits the rows into groups that look the same, each a line of the :number text.
(plot/render-image {:data iris
                    :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length :color :species}
                    :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :cohens-d})]}
                   {:width 450 :height 300})

Its options pick the statistic and how the chart draws and writes it. The chart colours a shade by its value on a colour scale over the panels’ values; a signed statistic takes a diverging palette centred on 0:

:colorDefault: [0 114 178] (theme); The theme's colour only for a shade when the plot maps an aesthetic; otherwise a shade shows the value on the colour scale. :number takes the :pairwise text role's colour, a group line its group's colour (the text's colour for :group).
(chart/pairwise {:glyph :shade, :color :orange})
One fixed colour for the shade or the :number text, in place of the colour scale. Text over a shade takes the :pairwise text role's colour, black or white by default.
  • solid colour
:methodDefault: :pearson
(chart/pairwise {:method :cohens-d})
Which statistic the chart computes, of the x sample against the y sample.
  • :pearson – Pearson correlation.
  • :cohens-d – Standardised mean difference over the pooled standard deviation.
  • :kendall – Kendall rank correlation (tau).
  • :rmse – Root mean squared error.
  • :spearman – Spearman rank correlation.
  • Other statistics of pairwise/statistics; the pairwise/matrix-rows section lists them all with what they measure. :ameasure, :canberra, :chebyshev, :cliffs-delta, :cosine, :covariance, :euclidean, :glass-delta, :hedges-g, :mae, :manhattan, :mape, :me, :means-ratio, :mse, :r2
  • function – A function of the two samples (f xs ys) that returns a number.
:labelDefault: The statistic's label, e.g. Pearson; value for a function.
(chart/pairwise {:label "r"})
The statistic's name in the text and the colour bar title; $...$ in it and plot/math values draw as formulas.
  • string – A text, e.g. r.
  • formula value – A plot/math or plot/runs value.
:glyphDefault: :number
(chart/pairwise {:glyph :shade})
How the chart draws the value.
  • :number – As text, the label and the value, a line per group.
  • :shade – The whole panel in the value's colour.
  • :shade-number – Both: the text over the shade.
:value-formatDefault: At most 2 decimals, whole numbers without them.
(chart/pairwise {:value-format "%.4f"})
The text of a value: a format string or a function of the number; a function that throws or returns nil shows NA.
  • number format

For one pair (sepal against petal length) the first three panels show the mean absolute error as text, as a shade and as both. The last three show the signed mean error on the diverging palette, a user function with its label and format, and one fixed colour:

(plot/render-image (plot/arrange [{:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :mae :glyph :number})] :labels {:title ":number"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :mae :glyph :shade})] :labels {:title ":shade"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :mae :glyph :shade-number})] :labels {:title ":shade-number"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :me :glyph :shade-number})] :labels {:title "signed :me"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method (fn [xs ys] (- (apply max xs) (apply max ys)))
                                                             :label "max diff" :value-format #(format "%+.1f cm" %)})]
                                   :labels {:title "function, :value-format"}}
                                  {:data iris :mapping {:x :sepal-length :y :petal-length}
                                   :charts [(chart/pairwise {:method :rmse :glyph :shade-number :color [120 160 90]})]
                                   :labels {:title ":color"}}]
                                 {:columns 3})
                   {:width 1000 :height 520})

In a plot/pairs matrix every cell shows the statistic of its column variable against its row variable, so the mirrored cells of the antisymmetric mean error have opposite signs - shaded below, as text above, the names on the diagonal (:diagonal :names). plot/pairs computes the statistic once for the matrix, so every shaded cell and the single colour bar use its range; the cells share one text size:

(plot/render-image (plot/pairs iris {:variables [:sepal-length :sepal-width :petal-length :petal-width]
                                     :lower (chart/pairwise {:method :me :glyph :shade-number})
                                     :upper (chart/pairwise {:method :me})
                                     :diagonal :names
                                     :title "mean error: shaded below, text above"})
                   {:width 700 :height 620})
